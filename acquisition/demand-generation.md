# Demand generation

## Why it matters

Some suitable buyers do not yet recognise the problem or prioritise it. Demand generation helps them understand the issue, its cost and the available approaches before they are ready for a sales conversation.

## Objective

Build familiarity and consideration among a defined market. Connect that work to future qualified demand without pretending every useful interaction produces an immediate lead.

## Framework

Problem recognition → Useful education → Confidence in the approach → Active evaluation

| Buyer question | Useful material | Distribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is this problem worth fixing? | Diagnostic with observable symptoms | Practitioner posts, partner sessions |
| What does a better process look like? | Worked workflow and trade-offs | Newsletter, webinar, community discussion |
| Why this solution? | Product demonstration with evidence | Site, sales follow-up, retargeting |

## Step-by-step process

1. Interview customers and sales about the event that made the problem urgent.
2. Select one market problem the product can credibly address.
3. Build a substantial asset from first-party expertise; avoid a lightly rewritten listicle.
4. Adapt it for the places the audience already uses. Assign distribution effort alongside production effort.
5. Offer a useful next step, such as a diagnostic. Do not require a demo request to read basic education.
6. Review audience response and qualified demand over a period that reflects the buying cycle.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

RelayDesk publishes an ungated guide to ownership gaps in inbound sales. A practitioner session walks through disputed territories using sample data. Follow-up offers the worksheet, with an optional routing review. The team watches relevant company engagement and later inbound conversations; it does not count every attendee as sales-qualified.

## Metrics

Use target-audience reach, meaningful content engagement, returning visitors and self-reported discovery as early signals. Review qualified inbound demand and opportunity creation over longer cohorts. Rising branded search is a useful observation, not proof that one campaign caused it.

## Common mistakes and checklist

Gating everything measures contact collection more readily than education. Publishing without distribution produces little evidence either way.

- [ ] One buyer problem anchors the programme.
- [ ] Content includes original insight or a worked example.
- [ ] Distribution owners and dates are assigned.
- [ ] Early signals are separated from revenue outcomes.

**Experiment idea:** compare a diagnostic-led session with a product-led session for similar invited audiences. Assess qualified follow-up interest, not just registrations.
