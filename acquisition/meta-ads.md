# Meta Ads

## Why it matters

The creative has to earn attention before the audience can evaluate the offer. Cheap clicks are useful only when they bring the right people into a buying journey.

## Objective and audience strategy

Choose the campaign objective for the action you want: awareness for exposure, traffic for visits, engagement for interaction, leads for enquiries, app promotion for app outcomes and sales for purchases. “Conversion campaign” describes a business goal; verify the current objective and conversion location during setup. See [Meta's objective guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/ad-objectives), which may require sign-in.

| Approach | Hypothesis | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad prospecting | Creative and conversion feedback can find suitable buyers | New-customer cost and buyer mix |
| Interest-led prospecting | A relevant interest gives a useful starting signal | Incremental value versus broad; actual delivery settings |
| Retargeting | A known visitor needs proof or an objection answered | Audience size, overlap, exclusions and frequency |

Interests are not proof of need. In automated audience setups, some inputs can act as suggestions rather than hard restrictions. Confirm which controls apply in the account. Avoid splitting a small budget across many similar audiences.

## Creative testing framework

1. Define one audience problem and one offer.
2. Write three genuinely different angles: a visible problem, a product demonstration and an objection answered.
3. Develop two hooks per angle. A hook opens the message; the angle is the reason to care.
4. Keep the destination and offer stable while testing the creative concept.
5. Use a platform experiment where available for a causal comparison. Unequal delivery in an ordinary ad set is directional evidence, not random assignment.
6. Advance concepts based on purchase or qualified-lead economics. Then refine opening frames, length and format.

**Illustrative example: D2C ecommerce.** Fictional AlderCarry sells a $90 work bag. One angle is organisation: “Your charger shouldn't live under your lunch.” Another is a product demonstration showing separate compartments. A third answers the fit objection by showing the bag on different adult users with actual dimensions. All claims need product evidence before publishing.

## Landing pages, lead forms and conversion campaigns

A purchase page should continue the ad's promise, show price, delivery terms, dimensions and returns, and make checkout work on mobile. Track purchases with consistent values; reconcile refunds and duplicate browser/server events if both are used.

For a considered service, compare an on-platform lead form with a website enquiry page. Native forms can reduce friction, but lower CPL does not prove better demand. Ask only qualification questions that affect routing or follow-up, and deliver the promised resource promptly. Meta describes website and form-based options in its [lead generation guide](https://about.fb.com/ltam/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/11/LeadGenerationGuide.pdf).

**Illustrative example: EdTech.** Fictional CourseHarbor tests a course-advice form against a page showing curriculum, price and time commitment. The primary metric is cost per attended, qualified counselling call, with submitted leads and contactability as diagnostics. No job-placement promise is used without evidence.

## Budget and metrics

For AlderCarry, a fictional $3,000 pilot might allocate $2,100 to a stable prospecting concept, $600 to one controlled creative comparison and up to $300 to retargeting if the audience can absorb it. Unused retargeting budget stays unspent until a useful opportunity exists.

- CPL = spend ÷ unique leads; use qualified-lead cost alongside it.
- Media-only CAC = spend ÷ new customers; full CAC includes acquisition labour, creative and tools.
- ROAS = attributed revenue ÷ spend. $4,500 revenue on $3,000 spend is 1.5× ROAS, not a profit calculation.
- Review contribution after product, fulfilment, returns and acquisition costs before scaling.

## Fatigue and optimisation

Falling response with rising frequency can suggest creative fatigue. It can also reflect audience saturation, seasonality or a weaker offer. Compare creative age, placement, CPM, click response and post-click conversion before deciding. Refresh the concept when the argument has worn out; a different background colour may not help.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Objective and conversion location fit the intended outcome.
- [ ] Customer exclusions and retargeting windows are intentional.
- [ ] Mobile page and lead delivery tested.
- [ ] Concept, hook and offer recorded separately.
- [ ] New customers separated from repeat purchasers.
- [ ] Frequency and quality reviewed alongside cost.
