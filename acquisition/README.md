# Acquisition

Acquisition earns its budget when it brings in people the business can serve at an acceptable cost. Start with the offer and the definition of a good customer before choosing a platform.

## Choose the job first

| Situation | Start here | Decision to make |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyers already search for a solution | [Google Ads](google-ads.md) | Which intent can we afford to capture? |
| The problem or product benefits from demonstration | [Meta Ads](meta-ads.md) | Which creative makes the offer relevant? |
| Specific companies and roles matter | [LinkedIn Ads](linkedin-ads.md) | Does targeting precision justify the economics? |
| Research and creative production are slow | [AI-assisted advertising](ai-assisted-advertising.md) | What can AI draft, and what needs verification? |
| Buyers are not actively shopping | [Demand generation](demand-generation.md) | What will help them understand the problem? |
| Interested people need a next step | [Lead generation](lead-generation.md) | What exchange is worth their contact details? |

## Working sequence

1. Agree on the ICP, offer, sales capacity and acceptable acquisition cost.
2. Choose one primary channel and one measurable conversion.
3. Test the landing page, tracking and lead delivery before spending.
4. Review lead quality with sales by source and acquisition cohort.
5. Reallocate money on mature evidence; keep an explicit test allowance.

**Illustrative example: B2B SaaS.** RelayDesk, a fictional lead-routing platform, starts with solution-aware Search traffic. It delays broader campaigns until sales can distinguish fit leads from students and vendors. Demand creation becomes a separate investment with a longer evaluation window.

## Launch checklist

- [ ] Offer matches the audience's buying stage.
- [ ] Campaign has one accountable owner and a spend ceiling.
- [ ] Duplicate leads and existing customers have a reporting rule.
- [ ] Sales has agreed on response ownership and qualification.
- [ ] A review date allows for the expected conversion delay.
