# ROI analysis

## Objective

Assess whether the commercial return justifies the acquisition investment. Revenue, contribution, cash flow and pipeline are different measures and should not share a label.

## Formula framework

| Metric | Formula | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ROAS | Attributed revenue ÷ ad spend | Revenue efficiency of media |
| Media-only CAC | Ad spend ÷ new customers | Media cost per acquired customer |
| Full CAC | Defined acquisition sales and marketing costs ÷ new customers | Broader acquisition cost |
| Contribution before acquisition | Revenue minus relevant variable delivery costs | Amount available to cover acquisition and fixed costs |
| Contribution-based marketing ROI | (Attributed contribution before acquisition − campaign investment) ÷ campaign investment × 100 | Return under the stated attribution and cost assumptions |
| Simple CAC payback | Full CAC ÷ monthly contribution per customer | Approximate months to recover acquisition cost |

Count each cost once. If contribution already deducts acquisition costs, do not subtract them again. The simple payback formula assumes stable monthly contribution; use cohort cash flows when churn or uneven receipts matter.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

Fictional RelayDesk's mature campaign cohort has $30,000 recognised revenue, $6,000 relevant variable service costs and $15,000 campaign investment: $12,000 media plus $3,000 allocated acquisition people, creative and tools.

- Contribution before acquisition: $30,000 − $6,000 = $24,000.
- ROAS: $30,000 ÷ $12,000 = 2.5×.
- Contribution-based marketing ROI: ($24,000 − $15,000) ÷ $15,000 = 60%.
- At ten new customers, full CAC is $1,500 for the stated cost scope.

These are invented teaching figures. The 60% result is an attributed estimate, not proof of incremental profit or total company profitability. Fixed overhead is outside this defined contribution calculation.

## Step-by-step process

1. Define cohort, revenue window and cost scope with finance.
2. Reconcile customers and realised revenue, including refunds where relevant.
3. Separate open pipeline, bookings, recognised revenue and cash received.
4. Use contribution and a full acquisition-cost view.
5. Show sensitivity to close rate, margin and attribution assumptions.
6. Make a decision using the acceptable payback and cash constraints of the business.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Revenue and costs use compatible periods and currency.
- [ ] Open pipeline is not treated as earned revenue.
- [ ] Cost allocation is visible and non-duplicative.
- [ ] Attribution uncertainty is stated.

**Experiment idea:** test whether additional spend produces incremental qualified demand before extrapolating average ROAS to a larger budget.
