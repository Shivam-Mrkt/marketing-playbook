# Account-based marketing

ABM concentrates sales and marketing effort on accounts where fit, potential value and a credible reason to engage justify the work. A named list is only the starting point.

## From market to pipeline

Market → ICP → Target accounts → Buying signals → Contacts → Messaging → Outreach → Pipeline

| Stage | Playbook | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define fit | [ICP framework](icp-framework.md) | Which businesses can we serve well? |
| Allocate effort | [Account selection](account-selection.md) | Which accounts merit which level of attention? |
| Build context | [Account research](account-research.md) | What do we know and what is only a hypothesis? |
| Establish timing | [Buying signals](buying-signals.md) | Why might this matter now? |
| Understand the committee | [Contact mapping](contact-mapping.md) | Who experiences, evaluates and approves the change? |
| Execute together | [ABM campaign](abm-campaign.md) | What coordinated action creates a useful conversation? |

## Operating rhythm

Sales and marketing agree on account entry, owners and exit rules. Review active accounts weekly for new evidence and next actions; review the account list monthly to remove stale priorities. Existing opportunities follow the sales owner's plan rather than a parallel cold sequence.

**Illustrative example: B2B SaaS.** RelayDesk selects fictional NorthstarOps, a 180-person software company expanding into Europe. Expansion is evidence of organisational change, not proof of a routing problem. Research and a conversation must establish the need.

## Checklist

- [ ] Fit and account potential justify the effort.
- [ ] One owner coordinates contact across channels.
- [ ] Signals are dated and sourced.
- [ ] Account progression is measured beyond impressions.
