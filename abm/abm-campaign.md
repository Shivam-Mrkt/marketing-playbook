# ABM campaign

## Objective

Coordinate a focused account programme that creates a credible business conversation and a qualified opportunity. Sales and marketing share the account plan, not just a list of names.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

RelayDesk and NorthstarOps are fictional. This is a worked campaign design, not a completed client campaign.

| Stage | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Market | B2B SaaS companies with inbound sales handoffs |
| ICP | 50–500 employees, supported CRM, multiple assignment rules |
| Target account | NorthstarOps, 180 employees; Tier 1 research priority |
| Buying signal | Public announcement of a European sales team |
| Research finding | Regional change confirmed; current routing problem unknown |
| Contacts | Revenue operations manager first; sales director and IT reviewer if relevant |
| Pain hypothesis | More territories could complicate lead ownership |
| Messaging | “How are you handling ownership rules as territories expand?” |
| Offer | Territory-rules checklist and optional workflow review |
| Pipeline gate | Confirmed problem, relevant stakeholders and agreed evaluation step |

## Step-by-step process

1. **Agree on entry and ownership.** Sales checks open opportunities and prior conversations. Marketing validates fit and the signal.
2. **Build the account brief.** Include sourced facts, hypotheses, contacts, offer and next action.
3. **Prepare role-specific material.** Give the practitioner a workflow example and the sponsor a business-case outline. Avoid invented ROI promises.
4. **Coordinate channels.** An initial relevant email can offer the checklist; a later professional-network touch can add context if appropriate. Paid account targeting supports eligible audience reach, not guaranteed exposure to every named person.
5. **Handle responses.** A reply, objection or opt-out stops the scheduled sequence. The owner chooses the next step from the actual conversation.
6. **Qualify before creating pipeline.** Confirm use case, fit, evaluation path and an agreed next action. A download alone is not an opportunity.
7. **Review weekly.** Discuss account movement, missing evidence and next action. Retire stale plays rather than repeating them indefinitely.

## Example sequence

Day 1: evidence-led email offering the checklist → Day 5: useful territory-rule example → Day 10: appropriate LinkedIn context → Day 16: close the loop if there is no response.

Timing is a planning example, not a universal sending rule. Relevant replies and channel preferences override the schedule.

## Metrics

Track accounts researched, relevant roles engaged, qualified meetings held, opportunities created and pipeline progression. Define engaged accounts through meaningful actions such as a substantive reply or attended discussion. Report sourced and influenced pipeline separately and count each opportunity once.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Account fit and signal verified.
- [ ] One owner coordinates touches.
- [ ] Message links evidence to a question.
- [ ] Content helps a specific buying role.
- [ ] Stop and qualification rules are agreed.

**Experiment idea:** compare the checklist offer with a workflow review within comparable accounts. Use account-level assignment so contacts from one company do not receive conflicting treatments.
