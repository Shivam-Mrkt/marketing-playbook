# Contact mapping

## Objective

Understand the buying committee and contact the right person with a relevant question. The most senior title is not always the best starting point.

## Role framework

| Role | What they may care about | Helpful material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practitioner | Daily effort, exceptions, usability | Workflow demonstration |
| Champion | Solving the problem and building support | Diagnostic and internal explanation |
| Economic buyer | Cost, value and priority | Commercial case with explicit assumptions |
| Technical evaluator | Compatibility, access and implementation | Accurate implementation information |
| Procurement or approver | Terms, risk and purchasing process | Clear scope and approved documentation |

One person may cover several roles. Record confirmed responsibilities separately from title-based guesses.

## Step-by-step process

1. Review known relationships and opportunity ownership in the CRM.
2. Identify the function that experiences the problem.
3. Find relevant public professional roles and verify current employment.
4. Map influence and approval responsibilities as hypotheses.
5. Coordinate outreach through one account owner; do not send five simultaneous pitches.
6. Update the map from conversations, including who should not be contacted.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

At fictional NorthstarOps, the revenue operations manager may own assignment rules, a sales director may sponsor the change and an IT reviewer may assess integration. RelayDesk begins with the workflow owner. If they confirm a project, the team asks who else needs to evaluate it rather than bypassing them.

## Metrics and checklist

Track relevant role coverage, active relationships and committee gaps at each opportunity stage. Counting contacts encourages list growth without improving understanding.

- [ ] Employment and role relevance checked.
- [ ] Confirmed roles distinguished from inferred ones.
- [ ] Existing relationship owner consulted.
- [ ] Next contact serves a clear account objective.

**Experiment idea:** compare practitioner-first and sponsor-first entry for matched account groups, measuring qualified meetings and relationship quality rather than connection counts.
