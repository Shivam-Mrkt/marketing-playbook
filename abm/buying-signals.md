# Buying signals

## Why it matters

Fit explains who might benefit. A buying signal helps decide when to investigate. A signal is evidence of a possible change in need, not permission to assume a purchase is imminent.

## Framework

| Signal | Possible implication | Verification question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant team hiring | Workflow volume or responsibility may change | What process is the new role expected to improve? |
| Geographic expansion | Territory rules may need revision | Does the expansion affect lead ownership? |
| New functional leader | Priorities may be reviewed | Is the relevant problem on the agenda? |
| Product evaluation request | Explicit interest in a solution | What outcome and timing matter? |
| Repeated relevant site activity | Possible evaluation | Can the identity and context be established appropriately? |

## Step-by-step process

1. Define which signals plausibly connect to the product's use case.
2. Record event date, source, account match and confidence.
3. Separate first-party requests from indirect or third-party intent estimates.
4. Check whether the event is still current and relevant.
5. Translate it into a question, not an accusation about a problem.
6. Expire stale signals and review whether they actually predict useful conversations.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

NorthstarOps' fictional expansion announcement is six weeks old. RelayDesk uses it to ask whether routing rules are changing. If a contact says the process is settled, the account moves to education; the announcement is not recycled as a perpetual urgent trigger.

## Metrics and checklist

Measure signal-to-conversation rate, opportunity rate, false positives and signal age at contact. Compare against similar fit-only accounts where possible.

- [ ] Signal has a source and date.
- [ ] Account identity is verified.
- [ ] Business implication is plausible and labelled as a hypothesis.
- [ ] Expiry and follow-up rules are defined.

**Common mistake:** treating every funding event as a software budget. **Experiment idea:** test one signal category at a time so a useful signal does not hide several weak ones.
