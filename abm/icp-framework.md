# ICP framework

## Objective

Define the companies most likely to benefit from the offer and support viable economics. An ideal customer profile describes an account; a buyer persona describes a person within it.

## Framework

| Dimension | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Industry and business model | A workflow the product actually supports |
| Company size and revenue | Complexity and plausible budget; unknown revenue stays unknown |
| Geography | Service coverage and practical buying constraints |
| Technology | Necessary systems and integration requirements |
| Pain points | Repeated, material problems the product can address |
| Buying triggers | Changes that may increase urgency |
| Decision makers | Roles involved in evaluation and approval |
| Disqualifiers | Requirements or economics the business cannot support |

## Step-by-step process

1. Review suitable customers, lost deals and costly poor-fit customers together.
2. Identify common operating needs rather than copying superficial traits.
3. Separate must-have criteria from preferences and timing signals.
4. Write explicit disqualifiers and a rule for missing data.
5. Ask sales and customer success to challenge the profile.
6. Test the profile against recent opportunities and revisit it as the offer changes.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

RelayDesk's provisional ICP is a 50–500 employee B2B SaaS business with a sales team, supported CRM and multiple lead-assignment rules. Revenue is useful if verified but not mandatory. A single-person business with no sales handoff is a poor fit; a 200-person company with unknown CRM use needs research, not automatic rejection.

## Metrics and checklist

Compare opportunity conversion, retention and service effort across ICP segments. A profile based only on closed-won logos can miss difficult implementations and poor retention.

- [ ] Must-haves have operational reasons.
- [ ] Fit is separate from intent.
- [ ] Unknown values are visible.
- [ ] Sales and delivery teams agree on disqualifiers.

**Experiment idea:** test one adjacent company-size band as a separate cohort before broadening the entire ICP.
