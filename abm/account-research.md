# Account research

## Objective

Find enough reliable context to propose a relevant conversation. Research should change the message or the decision to contact; a long dossier with no implication is wasted effort.

## Research framework

| Layer | Look for | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business | Product, customers, business model | Source and date |
| Change | Expansion, hiring, new leadership, operating changes | What happened, without inferred pain |
| Workflow | How the relevant job may be handled | Evidence and unanswered questions |
| People | Public role and responsibility | Current relevance and confidence |
| Message | A useful connection to the offer | Hypothesis and appropriate next step |

## Step-by-step process

1. Check CRM history and the sales owner before external research.
2. Read the company site and recent first-party announcements.
3. Inspect relevant job descriptions and public role information.
4. Separate facts from assumptions in the account note.
5. Write one business question the evidence makes reasonable.
6. Stop when there is enough context for the next action; investigate further only if the account merits it.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

Fictional NorthstarOps announces a European sales team. The account note says: “Fact: regional expansion. Hypothesis: territory ownership rules may need revision. Unknown: present assignment system and urgency.” RelayDesk can offer a territory-rules checklist and ask how ownership is handled. It cannot honestly say NorthstarOps is losing leads.

## Metrics and common mistakes

Review time per usable brief, evidence freshness and whether research improves relevant replies. Avoid personal trivia, unsupported technology guesses and copying an AI-generated profile without checking its sources.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Each factual claim has a public source or approved CRM evidence.
- [ ] Assumptions are labelled.
- [ ] Research changes the message or priority.
- [ ] Only relevant professional information is retained.

**Experiment idea:** compare short, evidence-led briefs with deeper briefs for similar Tier 2 accounts; assess qualified conversations per hour of research.
