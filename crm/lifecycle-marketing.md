# Lifecycle marketing

## Objective

Help people take the next useful step based on their relationship with the business. Lifecycle marketing extends beyond acquisition into adoption, retention and expansion.

## Framework

| Stage | Message job | Exit or transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New lead | Deliver what was requested | Resource delivered and interest understood |
| Engaged / MQL | Help evaluate the relevant problem | Sales review or explicit evaluation request |
| SQL / opportunity | Support the agreed buying process | Won, lost or recycled by owner |
| Customer onboarding | Help reach first useful outcome | Defined activation milestone |
| Active customer | Improve useful adoption | New need, risk or renewal milestone |
| Inactive / recycled | Check whether relevance has changed | Meaningful response or closure |

## Step-by-step process

1. Define stage entry and exit events in plain language.
2. Assign a useful message and accountable owner to each transition.
3. Separate requested service communications from optional promotional messages.
4. Suppress conflicting acquisition messages for active opportunities and customers.
5. Use observed needs and milestones rather than elapsed time alone.
6. Review progression, retention and negative feedback by entry cohort.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

After a fictional RelayDesk customer signs, the first milestone is a reviewed routing rule and a successful test assignment. Onboarding messages help complete that task. If it remains incomplete, a customer success owner investigates. Sending an upsell pitch before the core workflow works would miss the customer's immediate need.

## Metrics

Measure activation rate, time to first useful outcome, stage progression, retention and expansion with clear cohorts. Open rates cannot tell whether the customer adopted the product.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Stages have observable entry and exit rules.
- [ ] Each message has a specific job.
- [ ] Opportunity and customer communications are coordinated.
- [ ] Inactivity has a review path, not endless reminders.

**Experiment idea:** compare a task-specific onboarding reminder with a general product-tour message, measuring the same activation milestone.
