# Landing-page audit

## Objective

Find the most important obstacles between a relevant visitor and an informed next step. Prioritise problems using evidence, not personal design preferences.

## Audit framework

| Area | Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headline | Is the offer understandable immediately? | Read without navigation or ad context |
| Value proposition | Who is it for and what changes? | Audience and benefit stated plainly |
| Proof | Why should someone believe it? | Verified examples, product demonstration |
| CTA | What happens next? | Button and destination agree |
| Trust | Is the business credible and transparent? | Real identity, terms and support |
| Objections | What could stop evaluation? | Price, setup, compatibility, risk |
| Form | Does each field affect the next step? | Completion and error behaviour |
| Mobile | Can people read and act comfortably? | Real-device journey |
| Speed | Does loading interrupt the task? | Field data and direct observation |
| Tracking | Does the recorded event reflect success? | End-to-end test to CRM |

## Step-by-step process

1. Record audience, traffic source, offer and primary action.
2. Walk through the page and form on mobile and desktop.
3. Review relevant feedback and stage-level data.
4. Log each finding with evidence, business effect, owner and proposed change.
5. Prioritise broken tasks first, then supported message gaps, then lower-confidence design ideas.
6. Verify repairs; test uncertain improvements where traffic permits.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

RelayDesk's fictional ad promises territory-based lead routing, but its page opens with “A better way to grow.” The audit recommends naming the routing use case and showing one sample rule. It separately flags a mobile form error as an immediate repair. Only the message change belongs in the planned comparison.

## Metrics and mistakes

Measure qualified leads per eligible session, form errors and stage progression. A visual preference is not evidence of a conversion problem. Do not change the whole page at once if the goal is to identify which argument works.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Promise matches the traffic source.
- [ ] Proof is genuine and approved.
- [ ] CTA explains the next step.
- [ ] Mobile submission reaches the correct owner.
- [ ] Findings include evidence and priority.

**Experiment idea:** show a product workflow above the form and test whether qualified submissions improve without reducing appropriate enquiries.
