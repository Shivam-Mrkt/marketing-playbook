# Competitor analysis template

Use one record per competitor and compare the same customer problem across records. For live work, attach a source URL and observation date to every factual claim. Public advertising activity does not reveal spend efficiency or profitability.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

QueueHarbor and all observations below are fictional scenario inputs; there are no real competitor findings or source claims here.

| Field | What to examine | Completed example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | Identity and relevant product | QueueHarbor, fictional lead-assignment software |
| Target audience | Who the offer appears designed for | Mid-sized sales teams managing inbound enquiries |
| Positioning | Category and claimed distinction | Simple assignment rules for sales operations |
| Messaging | Repeated buyer promise | Clear ownership with fewer manual decisions |
| Channels | Observed distribution only | Scenario assumes Search ads, product guides and webinars |
| Offers | Resource, trial or sales CTA | Workflow demo and assignment checklist |
| Strengths | Supported advantages | Scenario product walkthrough makes the basic workflow easy to understand |
| Weaknesses | Evidence-based gaps, with uncertainty | Scenario page does not explain exceptions; actual capability remains unknown |
| Opportunities | Buyer need we could address credibly | Publish a transparent explanation of exception handling |
| Evidence status | Source, date and confidence | Fictional teaching inputs; live research must supply dated public evidence |
| Next action | Research or test | Verify real buyer interest in exceptions before making it the central angle |

## Review checklist

- [ ] Observation is separated from interpretation.
- [ ] Missing website detail is not called a missing product capability.
- [ ] No competitor performance estimates are presented as facts.
- [ ] Opportunity relates to a customer need, not imitation.
