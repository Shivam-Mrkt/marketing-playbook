# Cold email

## Objective

Earn a relevant reply from a suitable prospect. The first email should establish a credible reason to talk, not compress a full sales presentation into a paragraph.

## Framework

Verified context → Possible business implication → Useful offer → Small, clear CTA

## Step-by-step process

1. Choose a narrow fit segment and one relevant problem.
2. Verify the recipient's current role and the context used in the message.
3. Write an honest subject line that reflects the content.
4. Keep the opening specific; phrase unconfirmed pain as a question.
5. Offer a resource or short discussion that matches likely readiness.
6. Check sending eligibility, identity, contact details and opt-out handling before launch. Use the organisation's approved sending process.
7. Read replies and qualify the use case before pushing a meeting.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

All people and companies in this sample are fictional.

**Subject options:** “Territory rules at NorthstarOps” · “Lead ownership during expansion”

> Hi Maya,
>
> NorthstarOps' announcement about a European sales team caught my attention. Are lead-ownership rules changing with the new territories?
>
> I work on RelayDesk, a lead-routing product. We have a short checklist covering territory overlaps and unassigned leads that may be useful during the transition.
>
> Would it be useful if I sent it over?
>
> Arun, RelayDesk
>
> If this is not relevant, let me know and I won't follow up.

Arun is a fictional sender. A live message must use the real sender's required business details.

**Bad example:** “Re: our conversation. We guarantee 10× revenue. Book 30 minutes today.” It invents a prior exchange, makes an unsupported claim and asks for time without establishing relevance.

## Qualification and metrics

A positive reply is interest, not an SQL. Ask what process they use, what problem matters and whether reviewing it is a current priority. Track positive replies ÷ delivered unique prospects, qualified meetings held and opportunities. Keep bounces, opt-outs and negative replies visible.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Subject is truthful.
- [ ] Context is sourced and current.
- [ ] One offer and one CTA.
- [ ] No fabricated familiarity or performance claims.
- [ ] Suppression and reply ownership tested.

**Experiment idea:** compare a resource CTA with a problem-confirmation question, using comparable accounts and the same core message.
