# Follow-up sequences

## Objective

Give a relevant prospect a few useful opportunities to respond without turning silence into an endless chase. Each message needs a reason to exist.

## Illustrative example: B2B SaaS

This is a proposed sequence for fictional RelayDesk, not a universal sending schedule. Days are measured from the first message and adjusted for business days and local context.

| Timing | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Establish relevance | “Are ownership rules changing with the new territories? I can send a checklist.” |
| Day 5 | Add a concrete idea | “One rule worth checking: which owner wins when territory and named-account rules conflict?” |
| Day 10 | Answer a likely objection | “The worksheet can be used to review existing rules without changing tools.” |
| Day 16 | Close the loop | “I'll leave this here. If territory ownership becomes a priority, I'm happy to share the worksheet.” |

The later emails stay in the same thread when appropriate. Do not fake “Re:” to imply a conversation that did not happen.

## Step-by-step process

1. Define the audience, offer, maximum touches and owner.
2. Give each follow-up new substance; remove messages that only say “bumping this”.
3. Check suppression and replies before every scheduled touch.
4. Stop automation on any reply. A person handles interest, objections and referrals.
5. For “not now”, ask whether a specific later follow-up would be useful; do not assume permission for indefinite nurture.
6. Close the sequence without guilt, false urgency or invented scarcity.

## Qualification and metrics

Distinguish positive replies, polite declines, out-of-office responses and referrals. Measure qualified meetings held per delivered prospect and response quality by step. Opens are not a reliable trigger for escalating contact.

**Bad example:** “I've emailed six times. Are you serious about growth?” It pressures the recipient and adds no value.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Every step adds a useful detail.
- [ ] Reply and opt-out stops work across channels.
- [ ] Sequence has a final step.
- [ ] Timing respects business context.

**Experiment idea:** compare a three-touch and four-touch sequence with the same core offer, evaluating incremental qualified replies alongside negative response rate.
